Why HUD, why now

The AI race goes to whoever has the best data.

The last generation of companies won on apps. This one wins on data, and on the environments that turn it into better models.

Why HUD

The companies with the best data, or the best access to it, win.

HUD builds the environments, evals and marketplace that labs use to get that data. That puts HUD underneath the next generation of models, not beside them.

BD Lead · applied

Fill DataVendor's supply side

Most valuable data is private. Someone has to find the companies holding it and get them to list.

  • Build seller lists. Companies likely to hold codebases, tasksets and environments worth buying: sunset products, acquired startups, dev shops with years of history.
  • Open with the estimator. "See what your codebase is worth" is a better first line than any pitch I could write.
  • A brief before every call. The system researches the seller or lab and drafts a one-page brief. I check it, run the call, and log notes to the CRM after.
  • Run it to signed. Qualify, call, close, and track conversion at every stage so the playbook gets sharper each week.
Customer Ops · also open

Turn sign-ups into running environments

A team on the free SDK means little until it runs cloud hours, and grows into Enterprise.

  • Get new teams to a first run. Hands-on onboarding, weekly office hours and follow-ups until their first environment works.
  • Watch usage, flag expansion. Business reviews on the cadence each account needs, like I run at Uber.
  • Loop feedback to product. What blocks a team goes to the right engineer with evidence, not a vague ticket.
